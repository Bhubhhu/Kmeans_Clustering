Introduction & Problem Statement

Aggregate vaccination rates hide structural differences between national health systems

14M+

Zero-dose infants

Children who never receive a first DTP dose, even though global averages look high. Many more drop out between DTP1 and DTP3.

The gap

Grouping countries by region or income hides differences in multi-dose retention and newer-vaccine rollout (PCV3, Rotavirus).

The question

Can we group 194 countries on 11 immunisation indicators without arbitrary thresholds, prove the grouping is stable, and still interpret it responsibly?

Approach in one line

Standardise the data → compare K-Means (Euclidean) with K-Medians (Manhattan) for K = 2..8 → validate with 4 measures → project with PCA → interpret 4 coverage tiers.

Objectives & Need

Aligned with Immunisation Agenda 2030 and Gates Foundation / GAVI delivery priorities

Objective 1

Data preparation

Audit variable relevance, impute missing values by income-group median, encode categoricals, Z-score scale.

Objective 2

Clustering benchmark

Euclidean K-Means vs Manhattan K-Medians across K = 2..8 using WCSS, Silhouette, Davies-Bouldin, plus 2D PCA.

Responsible interpretation

Describe the tiers, test seed-to-seed stability (ARI), and document ecological and reporting biases.

Why it matters

Programme planners need peer groups defined by how delivery actually performs, so that GAVI co-financing, cold-chain investment and catch-up campaigns can be targeted by need.

Dataset & Preprocessing

194 countries · 11 numeric indicators · 3 contextual categorical attributes

Indicators

BCG, DTP1, DTP3, POL3, MCV1, MCV2, HepB3, Hib3, PCV3, ROTA and derived DTP_Dropout_Rate = (DTP1 − DTP3) / DTP1 × 100.

Missing values (share of 194)

Pipeline decisions

  • Impute with the median of the same income group, so fragile states are kept and not pulled toward rich-country values.
  • Scale to Z-scores so newer vaccines with larger spread cannot dominate.
  • Encode region, income and GAVI status by one-hot, but keep them out of the distance calculation. They are used afterwards to audit the clusters.
  • Result: zero nulls after imputation, checked by the test script.

Methodology: L2 vs L1 and Four Validation Measures

14 configurations: K = 2..8 × two distance metrics

K-Means (Euclidean, L2)

k-means++ initialisation, 20 starts. Centroid = mean. Minimises squared distances, so it is sensitive to large gaps between tiers.

K-Medians (Manhattan, L1)

Custom implementation with SciPy cityblock distance and component-wise median centroids. Robust to outliers in principle.

Validation measures

  • WCSS: compactness; look for the elbow.
  • Silhouette (−1..1, higher is better): cohesion vs separation.
  • Davies-Bouldin (lower is better): scatter vs centre distance.
  • ARI over 15 seeds (1 = identical every time): stability.

Why K = 4? An Honest Trade-off

Full validation table (Euclidean rows highlighted when selected)

Reasoning

  • K = 2 scores well but only splits "high" from "low" and hides the dropout and newer-vaccine patterns.
  • K = 3 has a slightly better Davies-Bouldin (0.67) but merges the lagging-newer-vaccine and dropout groups.
  • K = 4: WCSS elbow (768 → 261 over K = 2→4, then flattening), Silhouette 0.56, DBI 0.80, ARI = 1.00.
  • Manhattan collapses at K = 4 (Silhouette 0.24, ARI 0.69): correlated antigens make medians unstable.

Who Is in Each Tier?

Post-hoc audit using attributes that were not used for clustering

Takeaway: Tier 4 is entirely low-income and GAVI-eligible; Tier 1 is entirely non-GAVI. The clusters recover real structure from vaccination data alone, but Tiers 2 and 3 mix income groups and regions, showing that income alone is a poor proxy for delivery performance.

Limitations & Responsible Use

Statistical groupings, not permanent labels

Exploratory, not permanent

Clusters describe the current snapshot. Countries move as GAVI co-financing and cold-chain investment mature.

Ecological fallacy

National averages hide rural/urban gaps and conflict-affected zero-dose pockets.

Reporting bias

Administrative coverage depends on census denominators, which can be outdated in fragile states.

Data scope

The dataset is a prepared, WUENIC-structured table for method demonstration. Country labels in it are identifiers and should not be read as official country-level estimates.

Conclusion & Future Scope

What this project shows

Conclusions

  • Euclidean K-Means with K = 4 is the most defensible configuration: Silhouette 0.56, DBI 0.80, ARI 1.00.
  • Four tiers: 86 / 47 / 35 / 26 countries, ranging from DTP3 94.5% and 2.6% dropout to DTP3 42.2% and 32.7% dropout.
  • PCA keeps 90.8% of variance in 2 dimensions, so the tiers can be shown visually.

Future scope

  • Time-series clustering 2000–2025 (post-COVID recovery)
  • Gaussian Mixture Models for soft membership
